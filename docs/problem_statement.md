
# Problem Statement
## Health Monitoring Challenges During Space Missions

### 1. The Problem

Astronauts experience unique health challenges during space missions. Unlike people on Earth, they live and work in an environment with microgravity, radiation exposure, isolation, restricted living conditions, and changes in daily routines.

These conditions can affect different aspects of human health, making it important to monitor health indicators and recognize changes over time.

### 2. Major Health Monitoring Challenges

**Microgravity Effects**

Long-duration exposure to microgravity can affect the musculoskeletal and cardiovascular systems. Changes in bone health, muscle function, and fluid distribution require attention during space missions.

**Sleep and Circadian Rhythm Changes**

Artificial lighting, demanding schedules, and environmental conditions can disrupt normal sleep patterns. Sleep changes may influence alertness, performance, and general well-being.

**Radiation Exposure**

Space radiation is an important health concern beyond Earth's protective atmosphere and magnetic field. Monitoring radiation exposure is an important part of astronaut health risk management.

**Isolation and Confinement**

Living in a small, isolated environment for extended periods can affect psychological well-being, mood, stress, and social interaction.

**Limited Medical Support**

Astronauts operate far from Earth, where communication delays and limited medical resources can make health assessment and coordination more challenging.

### 3. The Monitoring Challenge

Health information may come from different sources, including physiological measurements, sleep records, environmental information, and astronaut self-reports.

When these data are reviewed separately, it may be difficult to understand their combined context or recognize meaningful changes over time.

A useful monitoring system should help organize these different types of information and make relevant changes easier to review.

### 4. The Need for ASTRA HEALTH

ASTRA HEALTH was designed to address this information-management challenge through an interactive platform that brings health indicators and symptom reports together.

The system aims to:
- Organize different health-related inputs in one interface.
- Support symptom reporting through an interactive body map.
- Present changes in available health measurements over time.
- Highlight selected patterns that may require further review.
- Prepare structured reports to support communication with medical teams.

### 5. Problem Definition

The central problem addressed by ASTRA HEALTH is:

**How can astronaut health information from multiple indicators and self-reported symptoms be organized, reviewed, and communicated effectively during space missions, where environmental challenges and distance from Earth make health monitoring more complex?**

ASTRA HEALTH explores a software-based approach to this problem through interactive monitoring, trend visualization, and structured health reporting.
